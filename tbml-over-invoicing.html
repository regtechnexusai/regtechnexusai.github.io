Over-Invoicing.

Following value, evidence and transaction context—not documents alone.

← Back to the Blog

Review caution: a price or value anomaly is a possible indicator, not proof of trade-based money laundering. Any escalation requires a documented, evidence-led assessment.

Why value context matters

Trade finance review can become too document-centric. A commercially plausible invoice may still sit inside a wider pattern involving counterparties, routing, product characteristics, payment behaviour, shipping information and changes in transaction value.

Over-invoicing is therefore best treated as a review prompt. It should lead to structured questions about the transaction and its context, not an automatic conclusion.

A practical review sequence

  1. Establish the expected commercial context: product, quantity, market, counterparties and route.
  2. Compare declared value with available evidence and relevant market or internal reference points.
  3. Review payment flows, timing, linked parties, amendments and unusual speed or repetition.
  4. Record what is known, what is missing and what would change the assessment.
  5. Escalate for qualified human review where the indicators remain material or unexplained.

Technology's role

Technology can help organise documents, surface patterns and prioritise review. It should not replace the accountable officer's assessment of evidence, applicable requirements and institutional policy.
