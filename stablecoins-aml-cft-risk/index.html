Stablecoins, Virtual Assets & AML/CFT Risk

A practical, source-led briefing for banking, compliance, financial-crime and risk professionals. Focus areas include stablecoin misuse, peer-to-peer transfers, unhosted wallets, transaction monitoring and regulatory boundaries.

Jurisdiction focus: Bangladesh, with FATF material used as international risk context. Source review date: 28 October 2026.

1. What the international risk guidance says

FATF’s March 2026 targeted report identifies illicit-finance risks linked to misuse of stablecoins, particularly peer-to-peer transfers through unhosted wallets. It discusses challenges involving cross-chain activity, offshore service providers, customer due diligence, technical controls and international cooperation.

Unhosted-wallet exposure

Transfers may occur without a regulated intermediary directly controlling both sides of the transaction. This can reduce the availability of customer and counterparty information.

Cross-chain complexity

Asset movements across chains, bridges and services can fragment transaction context. Monitoring should document the coverage and limitations of available analytics.

Offshore service providers

Assess jurisdiction, ownership transparency, licensing claims, sanctions exposure and the reliability of information obtained from external platforms.

Primary source: FATF Targeted Report on Stablecoins and Unhosted Wallets — 3 March 2026

Related source: FATF Seventh Targeted Update on Virtual Assets/VASPs — 2026

2. Bangladesh regulatory boundary

Bangladesh Bank’s FE Circular No. 24 dated 15 September 2022 addresses virtual assets and virtual currencies and prohibits transactions involving them as described in that circular. BRPD Circular Letter No. 40 dated 12 October 2022 directs scheduled banks to stop providing assistance for relevant virtual-asset/virtual-currency exchange, transfer or trading activity through customer accounts and to increase monitoring.

Bangladesh Bank FE Circular No. 24 — 15 September 2022 (PDF)

Bangladesh Bank BRPD Circular Letter No. 40 — 12 October 2022 (PDF)

Regulatory currency warning: These are identified primary instruments, not a certification that no later instrument or amendment exists. Before making a current legal or compliance decision, check Bangladesh Bank’s official circular register and obtain appropriate legal/compliance advice. Do not infer that a stablecoin is approved for use in Bangladesh.

3. Practical bank control review

Governance & policy

  • Document applicable Bangladesh Bank instructions and accountable policy owners.
  • Define escalation paths for suspected virtual-asset-related account activity.
  • Train relevant staff on typologies and the institution’s approved response.

Transaction monitoring

  • Risk-assess unusual patterns of rapid in/out transfers, repeated counterparties and unexplained third-party funding, without treating any single indicator as proof of wrongdoing.
  • Document scenario rationale, coverage, tuning and known blind spots.
  • Escalate and investigate under approved procedures; retain the audit trail.

Customer due diligence

  • Reassess purpose of account, expected activity, source of funds and counterparties where risk warrants.
  • Record corroborating evidence and unresolved inconsistencies.
  • Apply EDD and restrictions according to applicable law and policy.

Sanctions & investigations

  • Follow the institution’s applicable sanctions-screening and escalation process.
  • Preserve relevant records and avoid tipping off where prohibited.
  • Use blockchain analytics only within approved governance and validated limitations.

4. Audit-testing questions

  1. Can the bank demonstrate how the applicable Bangladesh Bank circulars are translated into policy, monitoring and staff instructions?
  2. Do sampled alerts show documented investigation, rationale, approval and disposition?
  3. Are scenario coverage, data quality, false-positive handling and model/scenario changes independently reviewed?
  4. Are suspected virtual-asset-related cases escalated consistently and supported by evidence?
  5. Are regulatory updates tracked, assessed for impact, assigned to owners and independently verified before closure?

Record the test population, risk-based sample rationale, evidence identifiers, exceptions, root cause, risk rating, action owner, due date and retest outcome. An alert or typology is a risk indicator—not, by itself, proof of a criminal offence.
