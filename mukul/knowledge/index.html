Six practical learning paths

These are educational starting points, not crop-specific prescriptions. Future editions should be reviewed for local climate, season, soil, crop variety and current official guidance.

🌱
FOUNDATIONS

1. Understand crop growth

Learn the broad stages of germination, establishment, vegetative growth, flowering, fruiting or grain filling, and maturity. Observe changes over time instead of judging a plant from one snapshot.

🪴
SOIL

2. Learn soil basics

Soil texture, structure, organic matter, drainage and pH influence plant growth. A soil test is more reliable than guessing nutrient needs from leaf colour alone.

💧
WATER

3. Observe water needs

Water needs vary by crop, growth stage, soil and weather. Check soil moisture and drainage, and look for both drought stress and waterlogging before changing irrigation.

🔎
OBSERVATION

4. Record plant symptoms

Record when symptoms began, which parts are affected, whether the pattern is spreading, recent weather and any recent farm inputs. Take clear photos from more than one distance; symptoms alone rarely prove a cause.

🧺
HARVEST

5. Handle harvest carefully

Harvest maturity, gentle handling, clean containers, suitable drying and safe storage help protect quality. Requirements differ across grains, fruit, vegetables and local climate; use crop-specific official advice.

📒
FARM PLANNING

6. Keep useful farm records

Track planting dates, variety, field location, weather, inputs, labour, harvest quantity, losses and sales. Consistent records help compare seasons and support traceability and business decisions.

Responsible-use note: Do not diagnose a disease or decide pesticide/fertiliser use from these general guides alone. Follow current official advice and product labels, and contact a qualified local agricultural extension officer when symptoms are severe, spreading or unclear.
