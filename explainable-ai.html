Explainable AI:
from model output to accountable understanding.

Explainable AI (XAI) is not simply about making a complex model look understandable. In a regulated environment, the objective is to provide decision-relevant explanations, preserve evidence, support meaningful human challenge and make the AI lifecycle auditable.

What is Explainable AI?

Explainability concerns how an AI system's operation or output can be represented and understood; interpretability concerns the meaning of that output in the context of the system's intended purpose. NIST treats explainability and interpretability as distinct but related trustworthiness characteristics and notes that explanations can support monitoring, documentation, audit and governance.

Regulated-industry principle: an explanation is useful only when its audience, purpose, timing and limitations are clear. A visually persuasive explanation is not automatically a faithful explanation of model behaviour.
01 · PURPOSE

Explain the decision

Identify what the model was asked to predict, classify, rank or recommend—and what decision the output supports.

02 · EVIDENCE

Explain the basis

Show the relevant input factors, data provenance, model/version context and material assumptions behind the result.

03 · CHALLENGE

Enable human review

Give reviewers enough context to question the result, identify uncertainty and escalate exceptions before consequential action.

XAI methods: choose for the use case

ApproachTypical useControl consideration
Intrinsic interpretabilityRules, scorecards, constrained or simpler modelsOften easier to document and challenge, but may trade off predictive performance or flexibility.
Feature attributionLocal or global contribution analysisConfirm what the attribution method actually measures and test stability; do not treat contribution as causal proof.
Counterfactual explanation“What would need to change?” analysisCheck feasibility, actionability and whether proposed changes respect policy and data constraints.
Example / case-based explanationNearest or representative examplesAssess similarity quality, privacy, data leakage and whether the examples are genuinely relevant.
Global model explanationOverall behaviour and important patternsUseful for governance and model review, but should not be substituted for case-level evidence where individual decisions matter.
Important: post-hoc explanations can be approximations. Validate explanation fidelity and stability rather than assuming that an explanation method reveals the model's true causal reasoning.

Explainability as a control system

For banks and other regulated institutions, XAI should connect to model governance rather than sit as a separate visualisation layer.

GOVERN

Ownership & accountability

Define model owner, business owner, control owner, reviewer and escalation route. Record the intended use and prohibited uses.

MAP

Context & impact

Map affected customers, decisions, data sources, downstream actions, jurisdiction and potential harms.

MEASURE

Explanation quality

Test fidelity, consistency, robustness, stability across relevant populations and usefulness to the intended reviewer.

MANAGE

Exception & remediation

Define thresholds, escalation, override, rollback, incident handling and change-management requirements.

AUDIT

Reconstructability

Preserve model/version, input snapshot where permitted, explanation output, reviewer action, approval and material evidence.

MONITOR

Post-deployment assurance

Monitor drift, data changes, explanation instability, override patterns, performance deterioration and emerging failure modes.

XAI testing checklist

  • Can the reviewer state what decision the model supports?
  • Is the explanation appropriate for the reviewer's technical skill and role?
  • Does the explanation remain materially stable under relevant perturbations?
  • Does the explanation have demonstrable fidelity to model behaviour?
  • Are important data limitations and missing variables visible?
  • Can a reviewer distinguish correlation from causation?
  • Are overrides and human challenges recorded and reviewable?
  • Can the institution reconstruct the model and explanation version used?
  • Have bias, privacy, security and adversarial risks been considered?
  • Are material changes subject to documented validation and approval?

Regulatory and standards context — 2026

NIST AI RMF: NIST identifies “explainable and interpretable” as a trustworthiness characteristic and frames AI risk management through GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE and MANAGE. NIST states that AI RMF 1.0 is currently being revised, so institutions should monitor the authoritative NIST source for future revisions. NIST AI RMF ↗

EU AI Act: The EU framework uses a risk-based approach and includes transparency, documentation, traceability and human-oversight requirements for relevant AI systems. As of August 2026, the Act's transparency rules are applicable, while certain high-risk obligations have later application dates. Applicability depends on the system, role and use case. European Commission — AI Act ↗

ISO/IEC 42001: ISO/IEC 42001:2023 provides requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an AI Management System (AIMS), including governance areas relevant to transparency, accountability and risk management. ISO/IEC 42001 ↗

Regulatory note: Explainability is not a universal legal requirement for every AI system. Its required depth should be determined by jurisdiction, applicable law or regulation, use case, impact, contractual requirements, internal policy and risk appetite.

RegTech Nexus AI approach

For regulated use cases, XAI should support the chain REGULATORY REQUIREMENT → USE CASE → RISK → EXPLANATION → HUMAN CHALLENGE → EVIDENCE → ASSURANCE. The objective is not to promise that every AI model can be made fully transparent; it is to establish proportionate, testable and auditable explanation practices.

Practical assurance question: if an auditor, supervisor, model-risk reviewer or affected decision-maker cannot understand what the system was intended to do, what materially influenced the output, what uncertainty or limitation existed, and who reviewed the outcome, the XAI control environment may be insufficient for the use case.
