Green Finance & Climate Risk in Banking

A practical review framework for banks, finance companies, risk teams, compliance officers and internal auditors. Use it to organise governance reviews, identify evidence gaps and challenge climate-related risk controls—not as a substitute for applicable law, the institution’s approved policies or professional judgment.

Jurisdiction focus: Bangladesh, with international reference material where relevant. Source review date: 28 October 2026.

1. Regulatory starting points

Climate Risk Management

Begin with Bangladesh Bank’s November 2025 Guideline on Climate Risk Management for Banks and Finance Companies. Map the applicable expectations to board oversight, risk management, business processes, monitoring and reporting.

Read the official Bangladesh Bank guideline (PDF)

Sustainability & Climate Disclosure

Review the institution’s disclosure governance, data ownership, methodology, consistency and evidence trail against the applicable Bangladesh Bank disclosure guideline and subsequent instructions.

Read the 2023 disclosure guideline (PDF)

Environmental & Social Risk

Assess how environmental and social due diligence is integrated into credit origination, approval, monitoring, escalation and portfolio reporting. Confirm the currently applicable version and amendments before testing.

Check Bangladesh Bank’s official circular register

Currency control: A circular’s publication date does not by itself establish that it remains the latest applicable instruction. Before relying on a requirement, check the issuing authority’s register for amendments, replacement instruments, effective dates and scope. This page is professional review support, not legal advice or a regulator-issued interpretation.

2. Governance and control review

Board & senior management

  • Approved responsibilities and escalation routes are documented.
  • Climate and environmental risks are considered in the institution’s risk appetite and relevant policies.
  • Management information reaches the appropriate governance forum with recorded decisions.

Risk identification & assessment

  • Physical and transition risk drivers are identified where material to the portfolio and business model.
  • Risk assessments document methodology, assumptions, data limitations and review frequency.
  • Material exposures are connected to relevant credit, market, liquidity and operational risk processes.

Credit & portfolio controls

  • Environmental and social screening and due diligence are evidenced where required.
  • Approval conditions, covenants, exceptions and monitoring actions are traceable.
  • Sector and geographic concentrations are considered using justified methods and available data.

Data, disclosure & assurance

  • Data lineage, ownership, quality checks and estimation methods are documented.
  • Disclosed metrics reconcile to source records and approved reporting.
  • Internal audit findings, remediation owners, deadlines and retest evidence are retained.

Sampling note: Select samples based on risk, materiality, portfolio exposure and applicable requirements. These are suggested audit procedures, not a prescribed regulatory sample size.

4. Suggested evidence register

For each test, record the requirement or policy reference, control objective, population and sample rationale, evidence identifier, test steps, result, exception severity, root cause, responsible owner, due date and retest outcome. Distinguish verified facts from observations, inferences, assumptions and unverified items.

Use a consistent assurance sequence: DISCOVER → INSPECT → VERIFY → TEST → CHALLENGE → ASSESS → RISK-RATE → REMEDIATE → RETEST → ASSURE.

B. Climate-risk governance self-assessment

For each control area, select the closest current position based on available evidence. “Yes” means evidence is available and the control is operating; it does not mean compliance has been independently verified.

1. Board and senior management responsibilities for climate-related financial risks are documented.

2. Climate risks are considered within strategy, risk appetite and relevant policies.

3. Material physical and transition risk drivers are identified across relevant sectors and geographies.

4. Risk methods record key assumptions, data limitations, ownership and review frequency.

5. Climate drivers are considered in relevant credit, market, liquidity and operational risk processes.

6. Environmental and social due diligence is evidenced in relevant financing decisions and monitoring.

7. Portfolio concentration by sector and geography is monitored using documented methods.

8. Scenario analysis or stress testing is considered where material and proportionate to the institution.

9. Disclosure metrics have documented definitions, data lineage, quality checks and approvals.

10. Findings have accountable owners, due dates, remediation evidence and independent retesting where appropriate.

Complete the questions and select “Generate gap summary” to see your self-assessment summary.

Methodology and authoritative references

  • Basel Committee — Principles for the supervision of climate-related financial risks: governance, risk identification, measurement, monitoring, reporting and scenario analysis.
  • NGFS — Guide to climate scenario analysis (updated guide): scenario objectives, exposure identification, scenario selection, impact assessment and communication.
  • Financial Stability Board — climate-related financial risk principles: international baseline and proportional application.

Interpretation limits: Results are arithmetic outputs and self-reported answers only. They do not quantify actual expected loss, establish regulatory compliance, assign a validated risk grade, or replace institution-specific scenario design, validated data, independent model validation or local legal review.
