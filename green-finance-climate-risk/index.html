Green Finance & Climate Risk in Banking

A practical review framework for banks, finance companies, risk teams, compliance officers and internal auditors. Use it to organise governance reviews, identify evidence gaps and challenge climate-related risk controls—not as a substitute for applicable law, the institution’s approved policies or professional judgment.

Jurisdiction focus: Bangladesh, with international reference material where relevant. Source review date: 29 October 2026.

PROVISIONAL PUBLIC PREVIEW — FOR PRELIMINARY SELF-ASSESSMENT ONLY. This independent decision-support prototype is not a validated climate-risk model, regulatory compliance score, capital estimate or substitute for applicable requirements or professional judgment. 2. Governance and control review

Board & senior management

  • Approved responsibilities and escalation routes are documented.
  • Climate and environmental risks are considered in the institution’s risk appetite and relevant policies.
  • Management information reaches the appropriate governance forum with recorded decisions.

Risk identification & assessment

  • Physical and transition risk drivers are identified where material to the portfolio and business model.
  • Risk assessments document methodology, assumptions, data limitations and review frequency.
  • Material exposures are connected to relevant credit, market, liquidity and operational risk processes.

Credit & portfolio controls

  • Environmental and social screening and due diligence are evidenced where required.
  • Approval conditions, covenants, exceptions and monitoring actions are traceable.
  • Sector and geographic concentrations are considered using justified methods and available data.

Data, disclosure & assurance

  • Data lineage, ownership, quality checks and estimation methods are documented.
  • Disclosed metrics reconcile to source records and approved reporting.
  • Internal audit findings, remediation owners, deadlines and retest evidence are retained.

4. Suggested evidence register

For each test, record the requirement or policy reference, control objective, population and sample rationale, evidence identifier, test steps, result, exception severity, root cause, responsible owner, due date and retest outcome. Distinguish verified facts from observations, inferences, assumptions and unverified items.

Use a consistent assurance sequence: DISCOVER → INSPECT → VERIFY → TEST → CHALLENGE → ASSESS → RISK-RATE → REMEDIATE → RETEST → ASSURE.

Complete the record fields before treating an export as an assessment record. Use “Not applicable” only where a reason is documented. This is a selected-control screening checklist, not an exhaustive Basel-alignment test.

Do not enter confidential customer data or sensitive personal information. This page does not save the assessment to a server.

For each control area, select the closest current position based on available evidence. “Yes” means evidence is available and the control is operating; it does not mean compliance has been independently verified. Record a traceable evidence reference for “Yes” responses and a gap, issue or action reference for “Partly” and “No” responses.

4. Risk methods record key assumptions, data limitations, ownership and review frequency.

Regulatory alignment and limitations

Methodology and authoritative references

  • Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS), Principles for the effective management and supervision of climate-related financial risks (15 June 2022): international principles on governance, internal controls, risk assessment, management and reporting. Q8 addresses climate-risk integration into capital/liquidity adequacy assessments and scenario analysis where appropriate; do not label this as BCBS climate Principle 5.
  • Network for Greening the Financial System (NGFS), Guide on Climate Scenario Analysis (updated edition published 13 November 2025): scenario objectives/scope, scenario choice, economic and financial impact assessment, and communication/use. Mapping: Q3, Q8 and Q9.
  • Bangladesh Bank, Sustainable Finance Policy for Banks and Financial Institutions (SFD Circular No. 03, 22 October 2023): use as a local reference point alongside the applicable climate-risk management and sustainability/climate disclosure instruments. Bangladesh Bank’s Sustainable Finance Department quarterly report through March 2026 lists this policy under the same number and date. Confirm later amendments before relying on a requirement.
  • Bangladesh Bank, SFD Circular No. 05, 23 November 2023: Formation, Management Procedure of Climate Risk Fund and Reporting of Half Yearly CSR Activities: the title is listed in Bangladesh Bank’s Sustainable Finance Department circular register and quarterly reporting. Check the original circular and subsequent instructions before treating any provision as currently applicable. The June 2026 quarterly report is linked for a more recent reference point.
  • Bangladesh Bank, SFD Circular No. 06, 26 December 2023: Guideline on Sustainability and Climate-related Financial Disclosure: use where disclosure governance, metrics, methodologies and evidence trails are within scope; verify later instructions before treating any requirement as current.
  • Bangladesh Bank circular register: SFD Circular Letter No. 01, 2 February 2025, Guidelines on Environmental and Social Risk Management (ESRM) for Banks and Finance Companies in Bangladesh: a later ESRM reference to consider alongside the original 26 June 2022 guidelines. Confirm the current applicability and any subsequent amendments.
  • Bangladesh Bank, SFD Circular Letter No. 01, 13 January 2026: Green and Sustainable Finance Reporting under Sustainable Finance Policy-2023 (official circular register): verify the circular letter’s full text before asserting that it revoked, replaced or amended any particular provision.
  • Bangladesh Bank, Policy Guidelines for Green Banking (BRPD Circular No. 02, 27 February 2011): historical foundational guidance for banks. Treat it as a reference to trace through later instructions—not as confirmation that every provision remains current; verify amendments and replacement instruments in Bangladesh Bank’s circular register.

Scoring method: evidence indicator (%) = (Yes × 1 + Partly × 0.5 + No × 0) ÷ number of assessed controls (Yes + Partly + No) × 100. “Not assessed” and justified “Not applicable” are excluded from the denominator and reported separately. This unvalidated, unweighted indicator is not an implementation score, compliance score or measure of overall control effectiveness. A high percentage can be misleading if material controls remain unassessed.

International references are guidance, not proof of local legal applicability, compliance or endorsement. Check each issuing body’s current publication and applicable Bangladesh Bank instruments before relying on a requirement.
