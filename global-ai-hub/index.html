01 · GOVERNANCE & ASSURANCE

Make AI use accountable, reviewable and controlled.

Practical governance themes for organisations developing, procuring or deploying AI systems and agents. These are assessment and decision-support themes, not a substitute for legal advice or regulatory approval.

01 / INVENTORY

AI System & Agent Inventory

Record purpose, owner, provider, model/version, business process, data categories, risk tier and lifecycle status.

  • Use-case register
  • Model and agent ownership
  • Material change history
02 / RISK

Risk Classification & Impact

Assess customer, financial, operational, conduct, privacy, cyber and third-party impacts proportionately.

  • Inherent and residual risk
  • Impact and dependency assessment
  • Risk acceptance record
03 / OVERSIGHT

Human Oversight & Authority

Define what an agent may read, recommend, initiate, approve or execute, including escalation and stop conditions.

  • Human-in/on-the-loop design
  • Approval thresholds
  • Kill-switch and exception handling
04 / DATA

Data, Privacy & Security

Document data provenance, permitted use, access, retention, transfer, security safeguards and incident response.

  • Data lineage and quality
  • Privacy and security by design
  • Access and retention controls
05 / PROVIDERS

Third-Party AI Risk

Evaluate vendors, cloud dependencies, subcontractors, model updates, concentration risk and contractual assurance.

  • Due diligence and assurance
  • Audit and information rights
  • Exit and continuity planning
06 / MONITORING

Monitoring, Testing & Audit

Set measurable performance, fairness, reliability and control tests, with evidence and remediation ownership.

  • Pre-deployment validation
  • Ongoing monitoring
  • Independent challenge and retest
Inventory→Classify→Assess→Control→Monitor→Review & Remediate
02 · INITIAL MARKET EDITION

United Arab Emirates: regulatory intelligence with jurisdictional discipline.

UAE coverage is organised by regulator and legal perimeter. The UAE is not treated as one uniform financial-services rulebook: applicability depends on the institution, activity, location and licence.

CBUAE · FEDERAL FINANCIAL SECTOR

AI use by licensed financial institutions

The CBUAE's Guidance Note on consumer protection and responsible adoption and use of AI and ML by Licensed Financial Institutions was issued on 11 February 2026. It addresses governance and accountability, fairness, transparency, data quality and privacy, continuous monitoring, human oversight, integration with existing frameworks and third-party risk.

Read the CBUAE Rulebook guidance ↗

DFSA · DIFC PERIMETER

AI adoption and governance in DIFC firms

The DFSA's 2026 AI Survey reported that 71% of surveyed authorised firms used at least one form of AI; 53% reported exploring, testing, piloting or deploying AI agents. The survey also describes variation in governance maturity and growing third-party and cloud dependencies.

Read the DFSA 2026 survey findings ↗

DIFC · FINANCIAL CENTRE INITIATIVE

AI-native financial-centre ambition

DIFC announced an ambition to become an AI-native financial centre, spanning legal frameworks, business environment, talent, ecosystem infrastructure and the district's physical environment. This is an institutional initiative, not a regulatory approval or endorsement of this Hub.

04 · EVIDENCE & ASSURANCE METHOD

From source to accountable action.

Our assessment approach preserves the distinction between regulatory fact, interpretation, evidence, control design and management decision.

Evidence classification

  • Category 1 — Direct: primary source directly supports the statement.
  • Category 2 — Corroborated: multiple reliable sources support the same point.
  • Category 3 — Analysis: reasoned interpretation or implication, clearly labelled.
  • Category 4 — Unverified: insufficient evidence; not presented as fact.

Audit and review methodology

DISCOVER → INSPECT → VERIFY → TEST → CHALLENGE → ASSESS → RISK-RATE → REMEDIATE → RETEST → ASSURE.

Outputs should identify source, date, jurisdiction, applicability assumptions, reviewer, evidence gap and next action.
