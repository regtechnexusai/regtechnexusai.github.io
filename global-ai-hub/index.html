Priority actions

Actions below demonstrate the format of a report recommendation. They are not instructions issued to an actual institution.

P1 · 0–30 DAYS

Establish accountable AI governance

Approve an AI governance charter, named accountable executive, committee terms of reference, delegated decision rights and escalation thresholds. Evidence: approved charter, minutes and responsibility matrix.

P1 · 0–30 DAYS

Complete the AI inventory and risk triage

Reconcile business-owned and vendor-provided AI use cases; record purpose, owner, model/version, data categories, dependencies, risk tier and review trigger. Evidence: reconciled inventory and signed risk assessments.

P1 · 0–60 DAYS

Strengthen model validation and change control

Define independent validation, performance and drift thresholds, material-change triggers, approval gates and rollback criteria. Evidence: validation reports, change records and monitoring outputs.

P2 · 30–60 DAYS

Close third-party assurance gaps

Review provider assurance, subcontractors, data locations, incident notification, audit/access rights, model updates, resilience and exit arrangements. Evidence: due-diligence file and contractual control matrix.

P2 · 30–90 DAYS

Operationalise human oversight

Document approval authority, human intervention, override logging, exception handling, fallback and stop conditions for each material use case. Evidence: workflow tests and decision logs.

P2 · 60–90 DAYS

Implement assurance and retesting

Map controls to evidence, assign issue owners, set target dates, test design and operating effectiveness, and retain independent challenge and retest records. Evidence: test scripts, results and closure approvals.

05 · EVIDENCE & ASSURANCE METHOD

From source to accountable action.

Our assessment approach preserves the distinction between regulatory fact, interpretation, evidence, control design and management decision.

Evidence classification

  • Category 1 — Direct: primary source directly supports the statement.
  • Category 2 — Corroborated: multiple reliable sources support the same point.
  • Category 3 — Analysis: reasoned interpretation or implication, clearly labelled.
  • Category 4 — Unverified: insufficient evidence; not presented as fact.

Audit and review methodology

DISCOVER → INSPECT → VERIFY → TEST → CHALLENGE → ASSESS → RISK-RATE → REMEDIATE → RETEST → ASSURE.

Outputs should identify source, date, jurisdiction, applicability assumptions, reviewer, evidence gap and next action.

National profile for banking, securities, insurance and other regulated financial activities. BaFin and Deutsche Bundesbank have defined supervisory roles; the German FIU is the central authority for suspicious-transaction analysis and reporting intake.

  • Identify the competent authority by institution type and supervisory task.
  • Map the German Money Laundering Act (GwG) alongside directly applicable EU rules.
  • Check BaFin circulars, guidance and current national implementation measures.

NETHERLANDSFINTECH / FINANCIAL SERVICES

Netherlands — DNB and AFM

DNB and AFM divide prudential, integrity and conduct-supervision responsibilities according to institution and activity. Official DNB materials cover DORA and AML/CFT; AFM supervises specified markets and firms, including investment and crypto-asset activities.

  • Determine whether DNB, AFM or both are relevant for the entity.
  • Map the Dutch Wwft and national supervisory guidance to the EU baseline.
  • Check licensing, registration and passporting conditions before market-entry conclusions.

IRELANDINTERNATIONAL FINANCIAL SERVICES

Ireland — Central Bank of Ireland

The Central Bank of Ireland is the principal financial-services authorisation and supervisory authority across a broad range of regulated entities. Its official DORA and AML/CFT materials provide national supervisory context for EU-regulated firms.

  • Check the Central Bank's authorisation and supervisory requirements by firm category.
  • Map the Criminal Justice (Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing) Acts and current amendments.
  • Separate EU-level requirements from Central Bank guidance and supervisory expectations.

FRANCELARGE FINANCIAL MARKET

France — ACPR and AMF

ACPR supervises banks, insurers and specified financial-sector entities; AMF supervises financial markets and relevant market participants. AML/CFT and conduct requirements must be mapped to the correct authority and regulated activity.

  • Identify ACPR versus AMF responsibilities and any joint or delegated arrangements.
  • Check the French Monetary and Financial Code and current national measures.
  • Do not treat authority guidance as interchangeable with EU regulations or legislation.
ACPR ↗ · AMF ↗
LUXEMBOURGCROSS-BORDER FINANCIAL CENTRE

Luxembourg — CSSF and Commissariat aux Assurances

CSSF supervises a broad range of financial-sector professionals and their AML/CFT obligations; the Commissariat aux Assurances supervises the insurance sector. CSSF publishes detailed AML/CFT and DORA materials, including national implementation and scope clarifications.

  • Map CSSF versus CAA by sector and legal status.
  • Review CSSF AML/CFT circulars and current DORA communications.
  • Consider cross-border branch and third-country entity scope separately.
CSSF AML/CFT ↗ · CSSF DORA ↗ · CAA ↗
POLANDCENTRAL / EASTERN EU

Poland — KNF and General Inspector of Financial Information

KNF supervises the Polish financial market; the General Inspector of Financial Information (GIIF) performs central AML/CFT functions. Polish AML obligations and reporting channels must be read with the relevant national legislation and EU instruments.

  • Separate KNF market supervision from GIIF AML/FIU functions.
  • Check the Polish AML Act and current implementing measures.
  • Validate local licensing, reporting and language requirements for each business model.
