Lesson 1 — Pinyin, initials, finals and tones

Chinese writing uses characters (汉字). Hanyu Pinyin represents Mandarin pronunciation with Latin letters and tone marks. Pinyin helps beginners decode sounds; it is not the Chinese writing system itself.

Four main tones and neutral tone

MarkExampleLearning focus
First: mā妈 — motherHigh, level pitch
Second: má麻 — hempRising pitch
Third: mǎ马 — horseLow/dipping tone; actual speech changes with context
Fourth: mà骂 — scoldSharp falling pitch
Neutral: ma吗 — question particleLight, unstressed tone
Important: these examples are for tone awareness, not interchangeable spellings. Tone changes meaning. Third-tone sandhi and other connected-speech rules mean the written tone mark does not always describe the surface pronunciation in every context.

How to practise

  1. Listen to a reliable Mandarin audio model for each syllable.
  2. Repeat while tracing the pitch with your hand.
  3. Record yourself and compare the tone contour—not just the vowel.
  4. Practise initials (starting consonants) and finals (vowel/nasal endings) separately before combining them.

Spelling rule: type tone-marked Pinyin accurately where possible (mā, má, mǎ, mà); tone numbers may be used as an accessible alternative during early drills.

Lesson 2 — Characters and stroke order

Chinese characters are built from strokes and components. Many characters include a semantic component, a phonetic component, or both; do not assume every character is a simple picture.

CharacterPinyinবাংলা অর্থPractice
人rénমানুষObserve the two strokes and their order.
口kǒuমুখPractise the enclosing stroke pattern.
山shānপাহাড়Notice the three vertical forms.
日rìসূর্য / দিনPractise the enclosing form and inner stroke.
好hǎoভালোRecognise 女 and 子 components.

Practise standard stroke-order principles: top before bottom, left before right, horizontal before vertical in common patterns, and outside before inside where applicable. Character-specific rules still matter; use a trusted stroke-order reference rather than relying on general rules alone.

Lesson 4 — Sentence structure

A common basic Mandarin pattern is Subject + Verb + Object. Mandarin does not conjugate verbs for person in the same way English does, but aspect markers, word order and context are important.

PatternExampleবাংলা ব্যাখ্যা
Subject + Verb我学习。আমি পড়াশোনা করি।
Subject + 是 + noun我是学生。আমি একজন শিক্ষার্থী।
Yes/no question particle你是学生吗？আপনি কি শিক্ষার্থী?
Negation with 不 (basic pattern)我不是老师。আমি শিক্ষক নই।

These are starter patterns; word choice, aspect, measure words and context require further lessons.
