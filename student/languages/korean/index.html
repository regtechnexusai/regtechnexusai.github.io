Lesson 1 — Hangul: consonants, vowels and syllable blocks

Hangul is the Korean writing system. Letters are assembled into syllable blocks; they are not usually written as a simple left-to-right string of separate letters.

Build syllable blocks

Examples: 가 = ㄱ + ㅏ; 나 = ㄴ + ㅏ; 무 = ㅁ + ㅜ; 한 = ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ. Vowel orientation determines whether letters are arranged side-by-side or top-to-bottom.

Learning rule: use Revised Romanization as a temporary reference. Learn to recognise Hangul directly because romanisation does not consistently capture every pronunciation detail.

Lesson 2 — Pronunciation, batchim and spelling

Batchim (받침) is a consonant at the bottom of a syllable block. Final consonants may be pronounced differently from their spelling and can affect the sound at the start of the next syllable.

  • Practise plain, aspirated and tense consonant contrasts with audio.
  • Learn the seven broad final-consonant sound categories in beginner pronunciation lessons; detailed rules require separate study.
  • Study liaison, nasalisation and other common sound changes as patterns, not as random exceptions.
  • Write syllable blocks in the correct spatial order; check spelling and spacing in complete phrases.

Lesson 4 — Sentence structure and politeness

Korean commonly uses Subject–Object–Verb order. Particles mark grammatical roles; verb endings communicate tense, speech level and politeness.

PatternExampleবাংলা ব্যাখ্যা
Topic + 은/는저는 학생이에요.আমি শিক্ষার্থী। Topic particle attaches according to final sound.
Noun + 이에요/예요학생이에요.শিক্ষার্থী (হওয়া)—polite present pattern.
Verb stem + 아요/어요가요.যাই / যায়—polite present; stem and vowel harmony matter.
Question intonation/pattern학생이에요?আপনি কি শিক্ষার্থী? Context and intonation help.

Do not mix speech levels casually. Formality and honorifics depend on relationship, setting and the sentence.
