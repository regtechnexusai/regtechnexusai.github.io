How to Learn from a Formula

Use each formula as a learning unit: understand its meaning, verify its conditions, apply it in a worked example, then retrieve and practise it independently.

1 · Understand

Identify the concept, formula type, symbols and mathematical meaning.

2 · Check conditions

Confirm domain, restrictions, units and angle conventions before substitution.

3 · Apply

Follow Given → Required → Formula → Substitution → Calculation → Answer.

4 · Retrieve

Close the answer and solve a fresh problem without looking first.

Worked Examples

Quadratic Formula

Secondary · Algebra
Given: x²−5x+6=0
Formula: x=[−b±√(b²−4ac)]/(2a)
Substitute: x=[5±√(25−24)]/2
Answer: x=3 or x=2

Pythagorean Theorem

School · Geometry
Given: legs 6 cm and 8 cm.
Formula: c²=a²+b²
Calculation: c²=36+64=100
Answer: c=10 cm

Power Rule

HSC · Calculus
Given: f(x)=x⁵
Rule: d(xⁿ)/dx=nxⁿ⁻¹
Apply: f′(x)=5x⁴
Answer: 5x⁴

Conditional Probability

HSC · Probability
Given: P(A∩B)=0.2, P(B)=0.5.
Formula: P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B)
Calculation: 0.2/0.5=0.4
Answer: 0.4

Practice & Self-Check

1 · Area

Find the area of a circle with radius 7 cm.

Answer: 49π cm².

2 · Explain

Why must a≠0 in the quadratic formula?

Answer: Otherwise the equation is not quadratic.

3 · Differentiate

Find f′(x) for f(x)=3x⁴−2x²+7.

Answer: 12x³−4x.

4 · Select a method

A right triangle has legs 9 and 12. Which theorem finds the hypotenuse?

Answer: Pythagorean theorem.

5 · Check a condition

Can the ordinary power rule integrate x⁻¹?

Answer: No; use ∫1/x dx=ln|x|+C.

6 · Coordinate geometry

For y=2x+3, identify slope and y-intercept.

Answer: m=2 and c=3.
