Foundation

Money Laundering

The process of disguising or transforming proceeds linked to criminal activity so their illicit origin is obscured. Placement, layering and integration are useful learning models, while legal definitions vary by jurisdiction.

Terrorist Financing

Concerns financing associated with terrorism-related activity and can involve funds from legitimate or illegitimate sources. The control objective is to identify and mitigate relevant financial risks.

Risk-Based Approach

AML/CFT controls should reflect identified ML/TF risks. FATF treats the risk-based approach as a central part of its standards and expects risks to be identified, assessed and understood.

Core Control Concepts

KYC / CDD

Understand customer identification, verification, purpose and intended nature of relationships, ongoing due diligence and risk-sensitive monitoring.

Beneficial Ownership

Identify the natural person(s) who ultimately own or control a legal person or arrangement, or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted, according to the applicable definition.

Transaction Monitoring

Institutions use rules, scenarios, analytics and investigation processes to identify potentially unusual activity. A red flag is an indicator for review, not proof of crime.

STR / SAR

Understand suspicious transaction/activity reporting and why escalation, investigation and reporting requirements depend on applicable law and institutional procedures.

Sanctions

Study sanctions screening as a financial-crime control and distinguish screening alerts from confirmed matches and legal conclusions.

Control Testing

Ask: What is the control objective? What evidence demonstrates operation? What exception was found? What remediation is required?

ML FOUNDATION

Placement · Layering · Integration

The traditional learning model describes potential stages through which illicit proceeds may enter the financial system, become obscured through transactions and appear integrated into the legitimate economy. Actual cases may not follow a neat three-stage sequence.

CONTROL PRINCIPLE

Red Flag ≠ Proof

A monitoring alert, unusual payment, price deviation, complex ownership structure or unusual device/payment pattern is a signal for review. It should not be presented as proof of money laundering, fraud or another offence without appropriate investigation and evidence.

PAYMENT SAFETY

OTP & Authorised Push Payment

An OTP can authenticate possession of a communication channel without necessarily proving the customer's informed intent for a specific transaction. Payment controls should consider social engineering, customer confirmation and transaction context.

DIGITAL FINANCIAL CRIME

Network-Level Monitoring

Where relevant, review relationships among customer, payer, beneficiary, account, device, IP, merchant/operator and transaction patterns rather than relying only on isolated transactions.

TBML

Trade Evidence Chain

Connect invoice, LC, packing list, certificate of origin, Bill of Lading, customs declarations, Incoterm, ownership and payment-flow evidence. A single anomaly is not by itself proof of TBML.

GAMBLING / PAYMENTS

Connected Payment Ecosystems

Multiple accounts, rapid deposits and withdrawals, common devices or counterparties and offshore or unlicensed operators may create indicators requiring proportionate review where relevant.

50-Question Money Laundering & AML/CFT Study Bank

Dedicated 50-question revision section. Click each question to reveal the answer. The wording is designed for student learning, revision, awareness, poster/carousel use and quiz practice.

Study note: distinguish facts, risk indicators and legal requirements. PEP status, shell-company status, high transaction value or a red flag is not by itself proof of wrongdoing.
1. What is AML?
Answer: Anti-Money Laundering.
2. What is CFT?
Answer: Countering the Financing of Terrorism.
3. What is KYC?
Answer: Know Your Customer.
4. What is CDD?
Answer: Customer Due Diligence.
5. When can EDD be relevant?
Answer: In higher-risk relationships or contexts.
6. What is a PEP?
Answer: Politically Exposed Person.
7. What is a beneficial owner?
Answer: The natural person who ultimately owns or controls an entity or arrangement, or on whose behalf a transaction is conducted, subject to the applicable definition.
8. What is an FIU?
Answer: Financial Intelligence Unit.
9. What is a shell company?
Answer: An entity with limited or unclear substantial operating activity that may be misused.
10. What is sanctions screening?
Answer: Screening relevant parties or transactions against applicable sanctions lists.
11. What is terrorist financing?
Answer: Financing risk associated with terrorism-related activity.
12. What is a risk-based approach?
Answer: Applying proportionate controls based on identified risk.
13. What is a red flag?
Answer: A risk indicator that may require further review.
14. What is Source of Funds?
Answer: The origin of specific funds involved in a transaction or relationship.
15. What is Source of Wealth?
Answer: How the customer's overall wealth was accumulated.
16. What is a customer risk rating?
Answer: The assessed ML/TF risk level of a customer or context.
17. Why may a PEP relationship require additional attention?
Answer: The applicable risk framework may require consideration of corruption, ML/TF and related exposure.
18. What is suspicious activity?
Answer: Activity that, in context, may reasonably trigger review or escalation.
19. What is layering?
Answer: Using transactions or structures to potentially obscure the trail of funds.
20. What is financial crime?
Answer: A broad field including fraud, money laundering, corruption and other financial offences.
21. What is a customer profile?
Answer: A set of identity, purpose, expected-activity and relevant risk information.
22. What is false identity?
Answer: Use of false, forged or misleading identity information.
23. What is a sanctions list?
Answer: A list of persons or entities designated under an applicable sanctions regime.
24. What is a high-risk jurisdiction?
Answer: A jurisdiction presenting heightened risk based on relevant AML/CFT, sanctions or other risk factors.
25. What is one responsibility of a compliance officer?
Answer: Supporting the compliance framework, escalation, monitoring and reporting processes.
26. What is smurfing?
Answer: Potentially dividing funds into smaller transactions to obscure movement or avoid detection thresholds.
27. What is a money mule?
Answer: A person whose account or access is used to receive or move another person's funds, knowingly or unknowingly.
28. What is a dormant account?
Answer: An account with no activity for a defined period under applicable rules.
29. What is an unusual transaction?
Answer: A transaction that is inconsistent with the customer's profile or expected activity.
30. Who is a high-risk customer?
Answer: A customer or context assessed as presenting comparatively higher ML/TF risk.
31. What is a virtual asset?
Answer: A digitally represented value that may be electronically traded or transferred, subject to the applicable definition.
32. What is a false positive?
Answer: A legitimate activity or party incorrectly identified as an alert or match.
33. Why is record retention important?
Answer: To support audit, investigation, supervisory and legal obligations.
34. What is geographic risk?
Answer: AML/CFT risk arising from relevant country or regional risk factors.
35. What is a transaction monitoring system?
Answer: A system using rules, scenarios or analytics to analyse transaction patterns.
36. What is a cash-intensive business?
Answer: A business in which cash transactions may represent a significant proportion of activity.
37. What is a front company?
Answer: A legitimate-looking business structure that may be misused to conceal other activity or funds.
38. What is an anonymous transaction?
Answer: A transaction where the relevant payer or beneficiary identity is not adequately known, verified or traceable.
39. What is a third-party transaction?
Answer: A transaction involving funding, benefit or control by a party other than the account holder.
40. What is a non-face-to-face customer?
Answer: A customer onboarded or maintained without physical face-to-face interaction.
41. What is a sanctioned entity?
Answer: A person or entity designated under an applicable sanctions regime.
42. What is a predicate offence?
Answer: Underlying criminal conduct from which proceeds may be generated.
43. What is a beneficiary account?
Answer: The account designated to receive a payment or funds.
44. What is correct about a high-value transaction?
Answer: High value alone is not suspicious; context and risk must be assessed.
45. What is compliance culture?
Answer: An organisational culture in which laws, controls, escalation and ethical conduct are followed in practice.
46. What is placement?
Answer: The potential introduction of illicit proceeds into the financial system.
47. What is integration?
Answer: The potential appearance of illicit proceeds as apparently legitimate assets or economic activity.
48. What is transaction monitoring primarily intended to do?
Answer: Identify activity that may warrant investigation or escalation based on defined risk indicators.
49. Why is beneficial ownership important?
Answer: It improves transparency about the natural persons who ultimately own or control an entity or arrangement.
50. Why should a red flag not be treated as proof?
Answer: Because an indicator requires contextual review, evidence and appropriate investigation.

Bonus Awareness Questions

Bonus 1.

Why should an institution document the rationale for closing an alert?

Answer: To make the review, evidence, decision and escalation rationale traceable and auditable.

Bonus 2.

Why can a legitimate transaction still generate an alert?

Answer: Monitoring models use indicators and scenarios that can identify legitimate activity as well as potentially suspicious activity.

Bonus 3.

Why must current legal thresholds be verified?

Answer: STR/CTR thresholds, reporting destinations, retention periods and sector obligations are jurisdiction-specific and may change.

Professional-use note: STR/CTR thresholds, reporting destination, record-retention periods, PEP/EDD obligations and other legal requirements must be checked against the applicable current law, regulator/FIU instruction and institutional policy.

Case Studies · AML/CFT in Practice

Use these scenarios to connect risk → control → evidence → escalation. They are educational cases, not legal determinations.

CDD · BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP

Layered ownership structure

A corporate customer has several holding companies across jurisdictions and account activity does not clearly match the stated business purpose.

  • Map ownership and control to natural persons.
  • Compare stated purpose with expected activity.
  • Identify EDD questions and documentary evidence.
TRANSACTION MONITORING

Unusual cross-border payments

A customer with low expected turnover suddenly sends multiple high-value payments involving new counterparties and jurisdictions.

  • Compare activity with the customer risk profile.
  • Separate alert indicators from proof of wrongdoing.
  • Document investigation, disposition and escalation rationale.
TBML · TRADE FINANCE

Trade-document inconsistency

Invoice values, goods descriptions, shipping details and payment flows do not align across a trade transaction.

  • Reconcile commercial and transport documents.
  • Identify plausible TBML indicators and information gaps.
  • Record source evidence before escalation.
DIGITAL PAYMENTS · FINANCIAL CRIME

Connected gambling/payment ecosystem

Several small deposits move through different e-wallets or payment accounts followed by rapid withdrawals to another person's account. Devices, IP addresses or network attributes may overlap.

  • Connect customer, payer, account, device, IP, beneficiary and operator information.
  • Compare activity with the expected profile.
  • Consider offshore or unlicensed operators where relevant.
TBML · UBO · INCOTERMS

Price anomaly with trade-document mismatch

A cross-border shipment shows a large price deviation together with potential related-party links and inconsistent invoice, shipping and payment information.

  • Verify beneficial ownership.
  • Identify the stated Incoterm and named place/port.
  • Compare goods, HS classification, quantity, quality and benchmark pricing.
  • Reconcile LC, invoice, packing list, Bill of Lading, customs data and payment evidence.
