Q&A · Why do Incoterms® matter in TBML review?

Q: Why should a trade-finance reviewer record the Incoterm used in a transaction?

A: Because the rule helps identify the contractual allocation of delivery obligations, risk and costs. In a TBML review, that context can help a reviewer test whether the shipment, freight, insurance, documents and payment pattern are commercially coherent. An Incoterm mismatch is a review signal—not proof of financial crime.

Q&A · FOB, CFR or CIF?

Q: If a Facebook learning case refers to FOB, CFR or CIF, what should a student verify first?

A: Verify that the transaction actually fits the sea/inland-waterway rule, identify the precise named port and delivery/risk point, then reconcile carriage, insurance, shipping documents and payment terms. Do not select a rule merely because it appears on an invoice.

Q&A · Does an Incoterm prove a transaction is genuine?

A: No. Incoterms® allocate specified contractual responsibilities; they do not establish the truth of the underlying price, quantity, ownership, commercial purpose or source of funds. Those matters require separate due diligence and evidence review.

Trade-Risk Learning Case · Price + Documents + Incoterm

A RegTech Nexus AI Facebook learning case described a hypothetical export with an extreme price difference and an import transaction involving a related counterparty. The lesson is not that a particular price variance proves TBML. The learning objective is to connect Incoterm + invoice + LC + Bill of Lading + customs data + UBO/related-party information + payment flow.

QuestionWhat the learner should inspect
Does the price make commercial sense?Comparable market data, product grade, quantity, unit of measure and historical transactions.
Does the Incoterm fit the shipment?Named place/port, mode, delivery/risk point, freight and insurance responsibilities.
Do documents tell the same story?Invoice, LC, packing list, certificate of origin, Bill of Lading and customs declarations.
Who is really connected?UBO, directors, addresses, counterparties, payment parties and related-party relationships.
Does the money movement make sense?SWIFT/payment data, payer/beneficiary, intermediary, timing and potential reverse or circular flows.
Facebook-to-Study Quiz 1: Which statement is correct?
An Incoterm can provide useful transaction context, but it does not by itself prove that a trade transaction is genuine or free of TBML risk.
Facebook-to-Study Quiz 2: A large price deviation is discovered. What is the appropriate learning response?
Treat it as a risk indicator requiring contextual review and evidence gathering—not as proof of money laundering.
Facebook-to-Study Quiz 3: Which evidence set gives the stronger trade-risk picture?
A connected view of contract/Incoterm, invoice, LC, shipping documents, customs data, counterparty/UBO information and payment flows.
