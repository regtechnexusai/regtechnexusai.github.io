Financial crime rarely presents itself as one isolated event. Risk may emerge from the way a business operates, how trade is arranged, what documents describe, or how money moves. This research environment helps professionals organise those signals and decide what evidence deserves closer review.

WHY THIS RESOURCE EXISTS

A signal is a starting point, not a verdict.

Unusual activity can have a legitimate explanation. A new supplier, a changed shipping route or a payment from a third party may reflect ordinary commercial arrangements. The reviewer’s task is to understand whether the facts fit the customer’s known business, the underlying trade and the applicable requirements.

Use the patterns below to structure questions, not to label a person or business as suspicious. The framework is designed for professional challenge, evidence collection and control review.

REGTECH NEXUS AI REVIEW METHOD

From an observation to a defensible review.

The method keeps the reviewer’s reasoning visible. It helps a compliance officer move from an initial concern to a documented assessment without treating a single indicator as proof.

01 · CONTEXT

Understand the activity

Establish the customer’s business model, expected trade, counterparties, product, geography and payment terms.

02 · INDICATOR

Describe what differs

Record the observable inconsistency or change. Avoid conclusions that go beyond the information available.

03 · CORROBORATION

Seek supporting evidence

Compare relevant contracts, invoices, transport records, customs information, account activity and reliable external sources where lawfully available.

04 · CONTROL

Test the response

Check whether customer due diligence, trade-document review, transaction monitoring, escalation and recordkeeping controls operate as intended.

05 · DISPOSITION

Document human judgment

Record the explanation considered, unresolved questions, reviewer rationale, escalation and next action under the institution’s procedures.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

When trade documents do not tell the same story.

A bank reviewer notices that an invoice describes a different quantity from the transport document. That difference may be an amendment, a partial shipment or a data-entry issue. It may also warrant further inquiry. The reviewer should compare the contract, amendment history, shipment evidence and payment terms before deciding whether the discrepancy is resolved or needs escalation.

The benefit is practical: the reviewer records what was compared, why the difference matters and how it was resolved. Another reviewer can then understand the reasoning without relying on an unexplained alert label.

CorroborationCheck contract, amendments and shipment evidence.

GLOBAL BY DESIGN · LOCAL IN APPLICATION
